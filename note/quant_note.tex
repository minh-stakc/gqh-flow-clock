{\LARGE\bfseries The Institutional Flow Clock}\\[2pt]
{\large A pre-registered test of pre-positioning for calendar-scheduled institutional flows}\\[4pt]
Minh Hoang (solo entry), University of Florida \quad$\cdot$\quad Gator Quant Hacks 2026, Systematic Trading\\
Code, data scripts and full audit trail: \url{https://github.com/minh-stakc/gqh-flow-clock}

\vspace{-8pt}

\sect{1. Summary}
Pension, target-date and balanced funds, index-benchmarked bond managers and institutions raising month-end cash must trade on calendars fixed by mandates, index rules and settlement plumbing, and they pay for immediacy when they do. We take the position two to four days ahead of these flows and hand it to them at the month-end close. \textbf{New here:} one ``flow clock'' combining three such flows; a natural experiment that moves the dash-for-cash window with each US settlement change (T+3, T+2, T+1); sizing the Treasury index-extension trade by the month's announced coupon issuance; and a volatility-invariant path statistic for trend. All were pre-registered before any backtest (commit \texttt{d20c9d5}); the last three failed in-sample. A logged, overfitting-tested search (\nconfigs{} configurations, probability of backtest overfitting \PBO) selected \textbf{rebalancing pressure (A) + Treasury month-end index extension (C) + time-series momentum}: in-sample (2006--2024) Sharpe \textbf{\ISsr} net of costs (\ISsrtwo{} at 2x costs), maximum drawdown \ISdd, factor alpha \ISalpha{} a year ($t=\ISalphat$), but a Deflated Sharpe of only \DSR{} under the pre-registered trial count. \textbf{The single out-of-sample run (Oct 2024--Oct 2026) returned a Sharpe of \OOSsr{}} (\OOSsrtwo{} at 2x costs, \OOSgross{} before costs): \OOSret{} a year, below T-bills. Both flow sleeves lost money (A even before costs); only the trend leg earned. The same rules on CME futures, at about a third of the trading cost, earned \IFCFsr{} in-sample and \IFCFoos{} out-of-sample.

\textbf{Who pays us, and why it is not arbitraged away.} We are not the flow's counterparty at entry: we warehouse the position for two to four days and become its counterparty at the month-end close, when a fund that must trade a fixed amount at a fixed time pays for immediacy (Figure 2). These funds are price-insensitive by mandate: changing a rebalancing policy, an index rule or the settlement cycle takes governance, not a trading decision; arbitrage capital stays limited because each window is a short, levered bet whose expected gain is small relative to its risk. It dies if the patterns are crowded after publication, the flows move earlier, or costs exceed the effect. The trend variant PCT (extending the author's earlier work) scales positions by $R = A/(L\sqrt{W})$, the mean absolute deviation of log prices from their chord over the mean absolute daily return: volatility-invariant, $\pi/8$ under a random walk.

\sect{3. Data and universe}
Total-return-adjusted daily ETF prices (Yahoo Finance), the 3-month T-bill (FRED \texttt{DTB3}), Fama-French 5 factors and momentum (K.\ French library, to Aug 2026), and every Treasury auction since 2002 with its announcement date (U.S.\ Treasury Fiscal Data). Replication: Databento GLBX.MDP3 CME futures (20 markets, including ES and ZN), volume-ranked front contract, returns always within one contract plus one round trip per roll; ES/ZN sampled at 16:00\,ET to match ETF closes (daily correlation with SPY/IEF \corrES/\corrZN). History 2005-01 to 2026-10-02; the brief's 20\%-or-2-years rule puts the \textbf{out-of-sample} window at 2024-10-03 to 2026-10-02 (\nOOSdays{} days, \nOOSmonths{} month ends). \emph{Survivorship}: broad ETFs that still trade (a mild survivor choice; DBC, SLV, UUP, HYG launched in 2006--07 and enter after 300 days) and futures; \emph{corporate actions}: total-return adjustment; \emph{missing data}: none after each fund's first print. An audit found that counting window days on the realised calendar leaked unscheduled closures (Hurricane Sandy, 2012) before they were announced; we fixed it (sleeve C's Sharpe moved from \CpreFix{} to \CpostFix).

\sect{4. Methodology}
\textbf{Protocol and audit trail} (git commit, UTC, 2026-10-03). (i) \texttt{d20c9d5} at 10:45: hypotheses, rules, variant neighbourhoods, selection rule, gates and the out-of-sample data lock; the first backtest in \texttt{results/trials.csv} is at 10:53. (ii) \texttt{06b22c9} at 11:02: Amendment 1 (ensemble search with overfitting controls). By then build agents had logged \nPreAmendRuns{} sleeve-level in-sample runs (\nPreAmendSpecs{} specifications), which the author had not opened; no ensemble had been run. (iii) \texttt{b054990} at 11:45: \texttt{results/selection.json} frozen with the trial log, which holds no out-of-sample row. (iv) 11:46: the out-of-sample window, which the loaders refuse to return unless unlocked, evaluated once (every row of \texttt{results/oos\_log.csv} carries that minute; commit messages were later edited, contents and dates unchanged, and \texttt{results/commit\_map.csv} maps the hashes in the logs). The log holds \nISruns{} in-sample runs, of which \ntrials{} are distinct specifications at 1x costs (the rest are 2x-cost repeats and post-fix re-runs); \ntrials{} enters the Deflated Sharpe.\\
\textbf{Execution and costs.} Signals use data up to the close of day $d$ and fill at the close of $d{+}1$ (ETF trend: next open). One-way costs: 3\,bp for SPY/IEF-class ETFs (several times the half-spread), 5\,bp sector/commodity/currency ETFs, 10\,bp otherwise, 30\,bp/yr to borrow shorts; futures 1\,bp (ES, ZN; only about 1.4 half-ticks) to 5\,bp; cash earns the T-bill; every result is repeated at 2x costs.\\
\textbf{Ensemble and selection.} Streams: sleeves A--E (D: FX hedge flows, Melvin \& Prins 2015; E: auction cycle, Lou, Yan \& Zhang 2013; both exploratory) and a trend leg (PCT or plain TSMOM). The search spans stream subsets, three weightings (equal risk, shrunk inverse volatility, Ledoit-Wolf 2004 minimum variance; expanding windows, lagged), volatility targets of 6/8/10\% and an optional drawdown brake. Rule: highest in-sample Deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey \& L\'opez de Prado 2014) among configurations passing G1 (Sharpe $>0$ at 2x costs), G2 (no year $>$40\% of P\&L) and G3 (median Sharpe of one-step neighbours $\ge$ half its own), provided the CSCV probability of backtest overfitting (Bailey et al.\ 2017; 16 blocks, \nsplits{} splits) is below 0.25.\\
\textbf{Verification.} Each module was implemented by an AI coding agent from the pre-registered text and attacked by a separate AI reviewer that searched for lookahead (decisions up to a random cut date must not change when every later price is replaced by a random walk), spec drift and bugs, and re-derived headline numbers with separate code. Two bugs were fixed before selection: the closure-calendar leak and an overlay that charged re-scaling costs on idle streams (an impossible $-$124\% day); the search was re-run. A clean clone with freshly downloaded data reproduces every number here.

\textbf{In-sample evidence.} Sub-period Sharpe \suba/\subb/\subc/\subd/\sube{} (2006--08, \ldots, 2021--24); best year \bestyr{} of P\&L; 90\% block-bootstrap interval [\bootlo, \boothi]. Factor loadings are small but non-zero: market \ISmktbeta{} ($t=\ISmktt$), momentum \ISmombeta{} ($t=\ISmomt$), Treasury duration (IEF excess) \ISbondbeta{} ($t=\ISbondt$, from sleeve C); $R^2=\ISrsq$. The pick ranks first of \nconfigs{} configurations (median \cfgmedian) and its one-step neighbours have median Sharpe \nbrmed, so it is not a knife edge; but in the CSCV the training winner's Sharpe fell from \cscvIS{} to \cscvOOS{} on the held-out half, a selection haircut of about 30\%. On the pre-registered count of \ntrials{} trials the best unskilled trial is expected to reach \SRzero, above our \ISsr, so the Deflated Sharpe is only \DSR: after the search, the in-sample Sharpe is not statistically significant. With the post-hoc effective count (\neff, eigenvalue participation, likely too low) it is \DSReff. Timing matters: shifting every flow window one day earlier/later gives \IFCshiftE/\IFCshiftL{} for the A+B+C composite, one extra day of delay \IFCdelay.\\
\textbf{What failed in-sample.} The settlement natural experiment: fixed T+3 windows beat the settlement-aware ones (\Bfixed{} vs \isB; \BtwoFixed{} vs \BtwoBase{} within the T+2 regime), so B2 is rejected. Issuance sizing added nothing to C (\Cq{} without it). PCT did \emph{not} beat plain TSMOM (\isP{} vs \isT; futures \PCTFsr{} vs \TSMOMFsr), so its kill condition fired. The information-discreteness variant (Da, Gurun \& Warachka 2014) beat TSMOM in-sample on both universes (\isI, \IDFsr), though the ETF difference's 90\% interval [\IDcilo, \IDcihi] includes zero. D and E failed (E is negative at 2x costs).\\
\textbf{Out-of-sample.} The gross Sharpe fell from \ISgross{} to \OOSgross: the signal weakened, and costs on \OOSto$\times$ turnover, which the in-sample edge had absorbed, made it negative. Contributions in-sample $\to$ out-of-sample: A \contAis{} $\to$ \contA, C \contCis{} $\to$ \contC, trend \contTis{} $\to$ \contT{} a year; factor alpha \OOSalpha{} ($t=\OOSalphat$). Implied gross Sharpe: A \grossOOSA, C \grossOOSC. Kill conditions did not trigger (Figure 2): IEF earned \CoosIEF{} bp/day over $[T{-}2,T]$ versus \CoosOther{} on other days from $T{-}10$ to $T{+}5$ (in-sample \CisIEF{} vs \CisOther), and the drift-signed bond-minus-stock spread over $[T{-}4,T]$ stayed positive but fell from \AisSpr{} to \AoosSpr{} bp/day. The settlement experiment, now entirely in T+1, failed again (B \oosB). Twenty-four month ends cannot separate three explanations: decay (the rebalancing paper appeared on SSRN in January 2025 and as an NBER paper in March 2025, inside the window; McLean \& Pontiff 2016), selection (Deflated Sharpe \DSR), and noise (a two-year Sharpe has a standard error near 0.7, though the result is in the bottom \rollfrac{} of the strategy's overlapping in-sample two-year windows, median \rollmed, minimum \rollmin). PCT was the best out-of-sample ETF stream (\oosP) but the worst futures trend variant (\PCTFoos); we do not switch to anything, since that would be selection on the test set.

\sect{6. Risk management}
\textbf{Limits.} One hard cap, gross exposure $\le 4\times$ NAV summed over streams, which is also the effective per-name limit: IEF reaches it in Treasury month-end windows (daily gross averages \grossmean$\times$, 95th percentile \grossp$\times$; the $3\times$ per-ETF cap applies to the IFC composite only). No single stocks, so sector limits do not apply. The cap bounds a 3-day $4\times$ IEF window at about \stressOne{} per one-sigma move (\stressTwo{} at two sigma); the worst in-sample month was \ISworst. \textbf{Pre-set de-risking.} Built in and tested: per-stream volatility scaling and an 8\% portfolio volatility target (lagged 63-day EWMA), so exposure falls after volatility spikes. The pre-registered drawdown brake (halve exposure while more than 10\% below peak) was searched but not selected: here it fired once, in mid-2009, and lowered the Sharpe from \ISsr{} to \ISsrbrake. \emph{Proposed after the out-of-sample run, untested}: suspend a sleeve when its kill statistic (Figure 2) is negative over 24 months, and skip windows containing CPI, payrolls or FOMC releases. \textbf{Factor, regime and tail.} Duration is the main macro risk (a long-duration bet three days a month). Out-of-sample the market beta rose to \OOSmktbeta{} ($t=\OOSmktt$) and the duration beta to \OOSbondbeta{} ($t=\OOSbondt$): the book was more directional than designed. The strategy made money in 2008 and 2020 (flight to quality) and in 2022, when stocks and bonds fell together. Daily skew is positive (\ISskew), so the flow legs are not short volatility, but returns come in jumps: daily kurtosis \ISkurt, best month \ISbest{} (February 2020).

\sect{7. Liquidity and capacity}
Square-root impact (Almgren et al.\ 2005): cost per trade $=$ fixed $+\ 1.0\times\sigma_{\text{daily}}\sqrt{\text{trade}/\text{ADV}}$, using trailing 63-day median dollar volume and the strategy's actual trades, starting from gross returns. Net Sharpe by assets under management:

The ETF version concentrates up to 4$\times$ NAV of IEF (median volume \$\dvIEF bn a day in 2019--24) into month-end windows, so it is an 18-year test of the effect, not a vehicle: its edge halves near \$5M. ZN and ES trade about \$\dvZN bn and \$\dvES bn a day, so the futures flow composite (not the exact submitted mix) keeps half its edge near \$1bn (in-sample \IFCFtwo{} at 2x costs). Turnover is high (\ISto$\times$/yr), so the edge must stay large to pay for trading; out-of-sample it did not, even in futures (\IFCFoos).

\sect{8. Limitations, deviations and next steps}
A and C come from papers whose samples overlap ours, so the out-of-sample window was the only independent test, and the strategy failed it. Daily bars cannot time intraday flow, and Yahoo revises adjusted history. \textbf{Deviations from the pre-registration:} Amendment 1 replaced the selection rule (the original rule would have submitted the IFC composite, \IFCoos{} out-of-sample); the pre-registered one-day-delay and window-shift variants were not re-run out-of-sample; the effective-trial DSR, the rolling-window comparison, the capacity window and the out-of-sample diagnostics are post hoc. \textbf{Next:} do not trade this as is. Before any of its data exists we froze a forward test (\texttt{FORWARD\_TEST.md}, git tag \texttt{forward-test-2026-10-03}): F1 the submission unchanged, F2 its CME-futures implementation (in-sample \FtwoIS, out-of-sample \FtwoOOS, computed only after the freeze) and F3 the information-discreteness trend on 20 futures (which trailed plain TSMOM out-of-sample, \IDFoos{} vs \TSMOMFoos). Only returns from 2026-10-06 count; target positions for that day are committed, and results are reported quarterly whatever they show.
